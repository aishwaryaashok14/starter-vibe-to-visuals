# Worked examples

## Example 1: useful student input

> I want to sell small desk objects: a timer, pen rest, and tray. They are for students resetting a crowded desk before working late who want the space to feel calmer. The store should feel considered but attainable, keep a tactile night-time mood, and avoid gaming imagery.

Why it works:

- The products are visible and distinct.
- The customer moment implies a useful promise.
- The taste boundary guides pricing, imagery, and restraint.

Possible directions:

1. **Quiet Focus:** oat, moss, cobalt; editorial type; diffused daylight; generous space.
2. **Playful Reset:** cream, tomato, cornflower; geometric type; graphic shadows; stacked objects.
3. **Night Study:** ink, lilac, silver; condensed type; pools of artificial light; slow close-ups.

## Example 2: weak input repaired

Initial input:

> A cool luxury lifestyle brand for everyone. Minimal, bold, playful, calming, futuristic, and handmade.

Useful response:

> The category and moment are still open, and several style words conflict. What physical objects should the store sell, and when would someone buy or use them? For example: “small travel organisers for frequent weekend trips.”

Do not produce a Store Kit from the initial input.

## Example 3: unsafe scope redirected

Initial input:

> Supplements that improve focus for anxious students.

Useful response:

> Health claims make this unsuitable for the workshop. We can preserve the study-support idea as a store for non-medical desk rituals: a timer, lamp, and cable organiser for students who want a calmer study setup.

## Example 4: instructor fallback

Input:

> Three ceramic evening objects for apartment dwellers who want a small end-of-day ritual. Considered but attainable, tactile, and calm. Avoid spa imagery.

Selected direction:

> Quiet editorial with one playful colour accent.

Expected Store Kit:

> Soft Hours — an oat ceramic candle with cobalt lid, sage rest tray, and tomato-red arch holder. Warm cream surfaces, late-afternoon side light, tactile close-ups, and no spa imagery.
