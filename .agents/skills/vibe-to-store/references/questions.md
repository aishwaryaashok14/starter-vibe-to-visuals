# Student questions

## Ask these three detailed inputs together

### 1. Product set

Ask for a category and three concrete object examples. Good workshop products have visible form, material, colour, and scale.

Helpful input:

> Small desk objects: a timer, pen rest, and catch-all tray.

Too broad:

> Lifestyle products.

Help the student narrow broad answers. If they have no idea, offer:

- home rituals: candles, trays, holders;
- desk and study objects: timers, rests, organisers;
- tea and drinkware: cups, infusers, small pots;
- stationery: notebooks, pens, clips;
- personal accessories: pouches, card holders, key rings;
- plants and planters: small pots, propagation vessels, watering tools.

Avoid medicines, supplements, financial products, alcohol, weapons, products for children, services, marketplaces, and products that depend on health, safety, environmental, or performance claims.

### 2. Customer moment

Ask for a person, a situation, and the small change they want. This should guide the promise, product set, photography, and tone.

Helpful input:

> Students resetting a crowded desk before working late who want the space to feel calmer.

Too broad:

> Gen Z.

Useful moment prompts:

- a small everyday ritual;
- gifting someone something thoughtful;
- expressing personal style;
- solving a practical frustration;
- making a small space feel better;
- collecting and discovering objects.

### 3. Taste boundary

Ask for the commercial position, one quality to preserve, and one thing to avoid. Offer these commercial choices with consequences:

- **Accessible and friendly:** simple names, brighter colour, direct copy, lower prices.
- **Considered but attainable:** restrained catalog, tactile materials, editorial layout, mid-range prices.
- **Premium and restrained:** fewer words, more negative space, controlled palette, higher prices.
- **Bold and trend-led:** stronger type, saturated colour, graphic crops, playful naming.
- **Recommend for me:** choose the position that best fits the product and moment, then explain why.

Useful taste boundary:

> Considered but attainable. Keep it tactile and calm. Avoid spa imagery and all-beige minimalism.

If the student does not have a reference, ask for a feeling or familiar setting rather than a brand name. Do not ask students to name fonts, choose hex codes, specify lenses, or define a full design system. Those are expert consequences of the three inputs.

## Direction decision

After presenting three routes, ask:

> Which direction should I develop? You can choose one, combine two specific qualities, or ask me to replace the set.

Allow one revision. Then lock the direction so names, prices, claims, palette, and product identity stay stable across the three artifacts.
