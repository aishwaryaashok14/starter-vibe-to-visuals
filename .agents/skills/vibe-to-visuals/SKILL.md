# Vibe to Visuals

Help the student make one good route choice, three useful input decisions, and one visual-direction choice. End with `STORE-KIT.md` or `DESIGN.md`, not a pile of speculative assets.

## Choose the build route first

If the student has already said what they are building, use that route without asking again. Otherwise ask one question: **Are you building a physical-product store, a software app, or a service experience?** Give a one-line example of each if needed:

- **Physical-product store:** three tangible items, such as a timer, pen rest, and desk tray. Output `STORE-KIT.md`.
- **Software app:** a digital tool with one core user action and visible success, such as planning and completing a study session. Output `DESIGN.md`.
- **Service experience:** a customer journey for an offering, such as comparing tutors and booking a first session. Output `DESIGN.md`.

An app sold as a subscription is still the software route. A service with a digital booking or discovery journey is the service route. Do not redirect either to a physical store. If the idea genuinely combines routes, ask which experience the student will build in this workshop and keep the others out of scope.

## Ask three detailed inputs together

Give route-specific guidance and a concrete example for each answer. Ask one follow-up only if a missing fact would materially change the concept; otherwise state a reasonable assumption.

1. **Core offer or experience.** Store: category and three visible objects with different shapes or scales. App: one user job, primary action, and observable success. Service: offering, customer goal, and the digital touchpoint that moves them toward success. A useful app answer is “a study planner that helps a student choose one task, start a session, and see what they completed”; “an AI productivity platform” is too broad.
2. **User moment.** Ask who the person is, what is happening when they arrive, and the small change they want. “A parent comparing tutors before booking a first session” is more useful than “busy parents.”
3. **Taste boundary.** Ask for commercial position, one quality to keep, and one thing to avoid. Offer accessible and friendly, considered but attainable, premium and restrained, bold and trend-led, or “recommend for me.” “Considered but attainable; calm and trustworthy; avoid stock-photo corporate blue” gives a usable constraint.

Do not make beginners choose fonts, hex codes, camera settings, or a fixed screen count before they have a direction. Help them narrow vague answers without replacing a workable idea.

## Propose and lock the visual direction

Propose exactly three directions that differ in more than palette. For each, explain what it signals, why it fits the user moment, and the specific palette, type, imagery or lighting, composition, material or interface treatment, motion, voice, and one risk. App and service directions should describe interface hierarchy and interaction character; physical-store directions should describe product presentation and material truth. Use examples as inspiration, not as a fixed menu.

Ask the student to choose one direction, combine at most two named qualities, or request one replacement set. Allow one revision, then lock the choice so later artifacts do not drift.

## Create the matching source file

- **Store:** Create only `STORE-KIT.md`. Include one promise; customer and moment; chosen direction; exactly three distinct products with names, descriptions, prices, materials, colours, silhouettes, crop hints, zoom, and approved factual claims; five named palette colours with hex values; observable typography, lighting, composition, material, motion, and voice rules; approved website, social, and three-beat video copy; non-negotiables and avoid list. Use one currency. Do not invent health, sustainability, safety, performance, origin, or certification claims.
- **App or service:** Create only `DESIGN.md`. Include one promise; primary user and moment; one core action and observable success state; one focused journey with every screen or state needed to understand and test it; purpose, hierarchy, primary action, exact CTA, important state, and success for each; five named palette colours with hex values; observable type, layout, component, imagery, motion, and voice rules; approved interface, social, and three-beat video copy; non-negotiables and avoid list. Use static or simulated data. Do not add authentication, payments, live integrations, unsupported outcomes, or extra features unless the student explicitly requests them. Do not force exactly three screens.

When this starter package is available, use `templates/STORE-KIT-TEMPLATE.md` or `templates/DESIGN-TEMPLATE.md` to format the result and check every section. Do not ask the student to create both files.

Stop once the chosen source file is complete. Build the site or prototype, social image, and video only when the student asks for the next artifact. The later route is store image → ChatGPT Sites → social → video, or `DESIGN.md` → Google Stitch → public Netlify URL → Pomelli social → video from genuine Stitch moments. The public URL is the Pomelli handoff; a contact sheet is not required.
